# Delivery checklist

The goal is a working owner-operated ordering setup with a finite handover. Sell the fixed scope only after confirming the restaurant fits it.

## Qualify

- Confirm the decision maker and current business operation.
- Walk through the existing customer journey. Look for a specific missing or unwanted step, not an owner's age or assumed technical ability.
- Ask why the owner wants the change. A deliberate phone-only process is not automatically a problem.
- Inventory the website, point-of-sale, providers, contracts, printers/tablets, internet reliability and staff workflow.
- Confirm one location, no more than 30 standard items, simple options and pickup. Quote exceptions separately.
- Do not promise a two-minute implementation. Track real hours from the first installation.

## Agree scope and total costs

Use 07-Client-intake-and-acceptance.md. Document menu items/options, provider, account owner, charges, payment handling, taxes approved by the owner, fulfillment hours, delivery date and acceptance tests.

Present at least the viable existing-provider option before recommending a switch. Disclose any expected referral compensation. Do not present a platform's free self-service signup as an exclusive paid benefit; the paid work is configuration, testing, training and handover.

Collect the first $250 only after agreed scope through the confirmed legal seller's established payment method. Do not create a new payment account or change payout banking for this pilot.

## Configure under owner control

- Owner creates/owns provider account and completes identity, bank and tax verification.
- Owner invites the implementer with the least access needed. Do not ask for shared passwords or recovery codes.
- Configure approved menu, prices, required choices, unavailable items, pickup windows, prep time, capacity and notifications.
- Link the approved provider page from the existing site with permission.
- Test without replacing the existing working order path prematurely.

## Acceptance

With the owner's authorization, prove:

1. A customer can choose an available item and required options on phone and desktop.
2. The final amount and pickup instructions match the owner's approved configuration.
3. An agreed test order reaches the actual staff destination.
4. Staff can acknowledge and fulfill it using the intended workflow.
5. Payment status, receipt and any separately authorized refund test are correct in the provider.
6. Closed hours and unavailable items behave as agreed.
7. Owner can change one item or opening time and locate support.

Do not charge a real card or place a real order merely to demonstrate the service without owner agreement. Test or sandbox results must be labeled accordingly.

## Handover

Provide the provider support link, owned account/domain inventory, menu/hours instructions, notification troubleshooting, approved fee schedule, test evidence and acceptance record. Remove implementer access when the owner accepts the handover or according to the agreed support arrangement. Do not remove a prior provider or delete data without explicit approval.

Collect the remaining $250 after acceptance. For 14 days, correct defects within the agreed setup. New menu work, additional channels and operational staffing are separate work.

## Learn before expanding

Record delivery hours, cash actually collected, direct costs, support time, owner acceptance and referral payout separately. Review the first completed installation before increasing volume. A $500 invoice or a potential referral is not money received.
