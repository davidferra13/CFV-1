# Booking and Payment Communication

> Rules for the transition from "yes, I want this" to "here's the money."

---

## After Verbal Acceptance

When a client says "yes" or "let's do it" or "sounds great":

1. Confirm what they're agreeing to (date, guest count, menu direction, price)
2. Explain next step: deposit to lock the date
3. Send payment link (or tell them it's coming)
4. Set timeline expectation: "Once the deposit's in, I'll start working on your menu"

## Deposit Communication

- State the amount clearly: "$[X] deposit to lock in [date]"
- Explain what it covers: "This holds your date and covers the initial grocery planning"
- State the deadline if applicable: "I'll hold the date for [X] days"
- Never pressure: "No rush, just let me know" is fine
- If they don't pay within the hold period, follow up once, then release the date

## Payment Reminders

- First reminder: friendly, assume they forgot. "Hey, just checking in on the deposit for [date]. Want me to resend the link?"
- Second reminder: direct but not aggressive. "Wanted to make sure we're still on for [date]. I've got a couple other inquiries for that night, so just want to confirm."
- Third: release. "I'm going to open [date] back up since I haven't heard back. If you still want it, let me know and I'll see if it's still available."

## Final Payment

- Send invoice with full breakdown after the event (or before, per chef config)
- Include: total, minus deposit, equals balance due
- Include grocery receipts if pass-through model
- Payment terms: due within [X] days (chef configurable)

## Cancellation Communication

Tone: empathetic but business-clear. Reference the policy without sounding like a lawyer.

- "Totally understand. Per our agreement, [policy applies]. Let me know if you'd like to reschedule instead."
- Always offer reschedule as alternative to full cancel
- Never guilt-trip
