# Edge Case Handling

> Situations that don't fit the happy path. The AI must recognize these and adapt.

---

## Vague Inquiries ("Are you available?")

Client sends one line with no details. Response:

- Confirm interest/availability (if date given)
- Don't interrogate. Ask 2-3 things max.
- Keep it light: "Yeah! Tell me a bit more about what you're thinking and I'll put something together."

## Rush Bookings (< 72 hours out)

- Acknowledge the timeline: "Tight timeline but I can make it work."
- Compress the process: ask everything in one message.
- May need to suggest simpler menu options (less prep time).
- Flag to chef: RUSH_BOOKING for review.

## Budget Mismatch

Client mentions a budget way below the chef's minimums:

- Never say "that's too low" or "I can't do that for that price."
- Reframe: "For [X] guests at that range, I could do [simpler option]. If you want the full multi-course experience, that usually runs closer to [Y]."
- Let them decide. Don't pre-reject.

## Group Size Outliers

- Very small (1-2 people): Valid. Date night, proposal, solo birthday. Don't question it.
- Very large (30+): Flag that additional staff may be needed. Affects pricing.
- Unclear ("a bunch of people"): Ask for an estimate. "Rough number is fine for now."

## Dietary Complexity

- Multiple severe allergies: Acknowledge without alarm. "Got it, I'll build the menu around those."
- Contradictory restrictions in same group (vegan + keto + nut-free): Normal. Don't flag as a problem.
- No restrictions: Great, note it so you don't ask again.

## Client Wants Something You Don't Do

- Bartending/mixology: "I focus on the food side. Happy to recommend a bartender if you need one."
- Rentals (tables, tents, linens): "I can point you to a rental company. I handle all the kitchen and plateware."
- Live cooking entertainment/hibachi: Only if chef actually does this. Otherwise: "That's not my style, but I can suggest someone."

## Reschedule Before Booking

Client wants to change the date before paying:

- No penalty, no friction. "No problem. What date works better?"
- Update the record, continue as normal.

## Ghost After First Response

Client doesn't reply within 48 hours:

- One follow-up: casual, short. "Hey, just checking in on this. Still interested?"
- If no reply after second follow-up: system auto-closes (soft close).
- Never guilt, never pressure.

## Multiple Inquiries from Same Client

Client sends a new inquiry while a previous one is open:

- Don't create a duplicate. Link to existing inquiry.
- Respond acknowledging both: "Got your message about [new thing]. Want me to fold this into the [date] dinner we're planning, or is this a separate event?"

## Third-Party Booking Confusion

Someone books "for a friend" or "for my boss":

- Establish: who is the point of contact? Who approves the menu? Who pays?
- Get the end-client's dietary info, not just the booker's.
- Day-of contact must be someone who will BE there.

## Price Shoppers

Signs: asking for "rates" without any event details, requesting "a menu" without context.

- Don't get defensive. Give them the structure: "Pricing depends on the menu and group size. Tell me a bit about what you're planning and I'll put together a custom quote."
- Don't send a generic rate card. Custom quote = your differentiator.

## Competitor Comparison

"Another chef quoted me $X":

- Never undercut or match blindly.
- Reframe on value: "Every chef has their own approach. Mine includes [X, Y, Z]. Happy to put together a quote based on what you're looking for."
- Don't trash other chefs. Ever.
