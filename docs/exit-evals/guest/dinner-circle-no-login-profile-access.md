## Scenario #41: Recover lost Circle profile token

**Original classification:** Bridgeable
**Reclassified to:** Reducible

**Why guest leaves:** Guest cleared cookies or switched devices, losing the `hub_profile_token` cookie that grants no-login Circle access. They open an email to find a recovery link, or need to type their email into a recovery form to get one sent.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- The guest's email (stored in `hub_guest_profiles.email_normalized`)
- Their profile token (stored in DB, never exposed to other users)
- Their group membership (in `hub_group_members`)
- The circle name and group token

**Data source?** No external source. All recovery data is internal to ChefFlow.

**Client-collaborative angle:** Not applicable. The guest owns their own identity recovery; no other participant needs to provide data.

**Physical reality:** Screen-based. Guest is likely on phone or laptop, opening email or typing into a form. No hands-free or print needs.

**Compounding:** High. Once recovery works reliably, the guest's profile, dietary info, preferences, event history, and group memberships all persist across sessions and devices indefinitely.

**Solution design:**

- `sendCircleRecoveryEmail()` in `lib/hub/profile-actions.ts` (lines 496-571) already exists: rate-limited (3 per 15 min), validates profile+membership, sends email with `/hub/recover/[groupToken]?t=[profileToken]` link
- `/hub/recover/[groupToken]/route.ts` already validates the token, sets the cookie (1-year `max-age`), and redirects to the circle
- Recovery prompt already exists in `hub-group-view.tsx` (line 144: `showRecovery` state, email input, transition)
- Gap: recovery flow is only exposed inside the circle view after access is denied. It should also be accessible from token-failure states on `/hub/g/[groupToken]`, `/hub/me/[profileToken]`, and `/hub/join/[groupToken]`
- Gap: no "magic link" login from email notification deep links (user clicks "View in Circle" but cookie is gone)

**Where it appears:**

- `/hub/g/[groupToken]` (non-member state, access-denied banner)
- `/hub/me/[profileToken]` (profile page when token cookie mismatch)
- Email deep links in circle notification emails
- Any circle page after cookie loss

**What remains as permanent exit:**
Opening email inbox to click the recovery link. This is a sub-5-second exit and immediately returns the guest to ChefFlow.

**Priority:** High frequency (every device/browser change) x Low effort (infrastructure exists) = P1
**Spec needed?** No. The infrastructure is built. Remaining work is UI: surface the recovery form from more access-denied states.

## Scenario #42: Receive Circle updates

**Original classification:** Bridgeable
**Reclassified to:** Bridgeable

**Why guest leaves:** Notifications about Circle activity (new messages, lifecycle events, menu updates) are delivered outside the app via email or push. The guest reads the notification in their email client or notification tray, then decides whether to open the Circle.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- Full notification preference system per member (mute, email, push, quiet hours, digest mode) in `hub_group_members` columns
- Throttled, grouped, and quiet-hours-aware notification engine in `lib/hub/circle-notification-actions.ts`
- Web Push subscription system in `lib/hub/hub-push-subscriptions.ts` (token-validated, no auth required)
- Email templates: `circle-message.tsx`, `circle-update-notification.tsx`, `circle-digest.tsx`
- Deep links back to circle in every notification email ("View in Circle" button)
- Circle-first notification pipeline in `lib/hub/circle-first-notify.ts` that posts rich cards to the circle and then sends short emails pointing back

**Data source?** No. Notifications are generated by ChefFlow's own event system.

**Client-collaborative angle:** The chef and client posting updates in the Circle are the source of the notification. No external data needed.

**Physical reality:** Push notifications are the ideal glance-moment interface. Email is the fallback for members without push enabled. Both are standard screen interactions.

**Compounding:** Medium. Notification preferences are set once and serve every future circle interaction. But the notification itself is transient, not knowledge that compounds.

**Solution design:**

- Already built: email notifications with throttle/cooldown (5 min per circle), smart grouping (2 min batch window), quiet hours, digest mode (instant/hourly/daily)
- Already built: Web Push subscriptions via `saveHubPushSubscription()`, delivered by `sendPushNotification()` in the notification loop
- Already built: `NotificationPreferences` component with mute toggle, email/push toggles, quiet hours, and digest mode selection
- Gap: Push prompt (`HubPushPrompt` component exists) could be more prominent on first visit
- Gap: Email deep links do not auto-recover expired cookies (see #41 overlap)

**Where it appears:**

- Email inbox (circle message and update notification emails)
- Device notification tray (Web Push)
- `/hub/g/[groupToken]` notification preferences tab

**What remains as permanent exit:**
The notification channel (email inbox, push tray) is external by definition. Guest always "leaves" briefly to see the notification, then returns. This is inherent to any notification system.

**Priority:** Medium frequency (every circle update) x Low effort (fully built) = P2
**Spec needed?** No. System is comprehensive. Only gap is the cookie-recovery-on-deep-link overlap with #41.

## Scenario #43: Continue a Circle conversation from email

**Original classification:** Bridgeable
**Reclassified to:** Partially Reducible

**Why guest leaves:** Guest receives a circle message notification via email. Rather than clicking through to the Circle portal, they hit "Reply" in their email client because it is faster and more natural (especially on mobile).

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- Email-to-circle ingestion system in `lib/hub/email-to-circle.ts`
- `routeEmailReplyToCircle()` function that: finds or creates a hub guest profile for the sender, cleans email body (strips signatures, quoted text, HTML), and inserts the reply as a `hub_messages` row with `source: 'email'`
- Profile dedup by normalized email (`findOrCreateClientHubProfile()`)
- Membership auto-creation for the sender if not already a member
- "via email" badge support in the Circle UI for messages sourced from email
- Gmail sync integration that identifies inquiry thread replies and routes them

**Data source?** The email provider (Gmail via sync) is the data source. ChefFlow already drinks from it via `lib/gmail/sync.ts`.

**Client-collaborative angle:** The guest replying via email IS the collaboration. The system should just capture their reply without requiring them to switch tools.

**Physical reality:** Email reply is the fastest possible interface for a guest who is already reading a notification. No friction, no context switch needed.

**Compounding:** Medium. The reply becomes part of the permanent circle conversation history. Future circles with the same guest benefit from the established profile.

**Solution design:**

- Already built: `routeEmailReplyToCircle()` parses and inserts email replies
- Already built: Gmail sync identifies inquiry-linked threads and routes replies
- Gap: Only works for inquiry-linked circles (relies on `getCircleForContext` finding a match via inquiryId or eventId). Community circles or circles without linked inquiries cannot receive email replies
- Gap: The notification email does not include a reply-to address that routes back to the circle (would need a unique per-circle inbound address or webhook-based email processing)
- Gap: Members without Gmail sync (i.e., non-chef members) cannot reply via email unless the system processes inbound email webhooks (SendGrid, Mailgun, etc.)

**Where it appears:**

- Email client (reply action)
- Circle chat (message appears with "via email" badge)
- Gmail sync pipeline (processing layer)

**What remains as permanent exit:**
The email client is where the guest reads and composes the reply. That exit is permanent. But the RETURN of the message content to ChefFlow can be automated (partially reducible for inquiry-linked circles, not yet for community circles).

**Priority:** Medium frequency (guests prefer email reply) x Medium effort (inbound email processing needed for full coverage) = P1
**Spec needed?** Yes, for extending email-to-circle beyond inquiry-linked circles (inbound email webhook processing).

## Scenario #44: Create a community circle as anonymous visitor

**Original classification:** Reducible
**Reclassified to:** Reducible

**Why guest leaves:** Anonymous visitor on `/hub/circles` wants to create a community circle but sees "Sign in to create" button that redirects to `/auth/signin`. The creation action requires a full account because circle ownership needs a persistent identity for moderation, content management, and abuse prevention.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- `CirclesDiscoveryView` component (line 95-109) explicitly checks `isAuthenticated` and shows either the create button or the sign-in redirect
- `CreateCommunityCircleForm` component exists and works for authenticated users
- The browsing, searching, and joining of public circles does NOT require auth
- Hub guest profiles provide lightweight no-login identity for joining existing circles

**Data source?** No external source. This is an auth policy decision.

**Client-collaborative angle:** Not applicable. The anonymous visitor wants to create, not collaborate on an existing circle.

**Physical reality:** Screen-based form interaction. No special considerations.

**Compounding:** High. A created circle becomes a permanent community asset. But requiring auth for creation is a reasonable trust gate.

**Solution design:**

- The current behavior (auth required for creation, not for joining) is actually correct product design: circle owners need accountability for moderation
- Reducible approach: preserve the visitor's intended topic/name/description in URL params or localStorage, then after sign-in/sign-up redirect them back to the creation flow with fields pre-filled
- Gap: Currently the sign-in redirect does NOT preserve the user's intended circle creation context. They lose their momentum
- Gap: No "quick account" flow (email-only signup) that is lighter than full OAuth/password
- Alternative: Allow creation with just email verification (similar to hub profile token model) without full account, but this creates moderation risk

**Where it appears:**

- `/hub/circles` (community circles discovery page)
- `/auth/signin` (redirect target)

**What remains as permanent exit:**
The auth wall itself. Some form of identity verification for circle creation is necessary. But the friction can be reduced to near-zero with preserved intent and streamlined sign-up.

**Priority:** Low frequency (few anonymous visitors create circles vs. join them) x Medium effort (preserve-intent redirect) = P2
**Spec needed?** No. Small UX improvement: add `?redirect=/hub/circles&intent=create&topic=X` params to the sign-in link.

## Scenario #45: Invite someone by SMS

**Original classification:** Bridgeable
**Reclassified to:** Bridgeable

**Why guest leaves:** A circle member wants to invite another person (friend, family member, guest) to the Dinner Circle. They need to open their native SMS app to send the invite link because ChefFlow cannot programmatically send an SMS on behalf of the member without their phone.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- `CircleInviteCard` component with full invite workflow: copy link, copy message, native Share API, and `sms:?&body=` deep link
- `buildCircleInviteShareMessage()` in `lib/hub/invite-copy.ts` generates role-aware invite text (chef/client/member variants)
- `buildSmsHref()` in the invite card generates an `sms:` protocol URL that pre-fills the message body
- `getHubInviteLinkContext()` generates attribution-signed invite URLs for tracking who invited whom
- Native Web Share API integration (`navigator.share`) as primary action, SMS as explicit secondary
- Role-specific copy pills explaining the link's purpose

**Data source?** No. The invite URL and message are generated internally. The SMS channel is native device capability.

**Client-collaborative angle:** The inviting member IS the collaborator. They choose who to invite and use their own contact list and messaging app.

**Physical reality:** Native SMS compose is the correct UX. Users expect to send personal invites from their own messaging apps, not from a third-party platform.

**Compounding:** Medium. Each invite potentially adds a permanent circle member whose preferences and participation compound over time.

**Solution design:**

- Already built: `sms:?&body=` deep link pre-fills the invite message in the native SMS composer
- Already built: role-aware message templates ("I set up our Dinner Circle... Use this link...")
- Already built: Web Share API as primary action (handles SMS, WhatsApp, email, etc. through OS share sheet)
- Already built: clipboard fallback for copying the message or link independently
- Already built: invite attribution tokens for tracking referral chains
- Gap: No server-side SMS sending option for the chef to invite guests directly (Twilio integration exists in `lib/sms/send.ts` but is not wired to circle invites)
- Gap: No bulk invite from a guest list or contact import

**Where it appears:**

- Circle invite card within `/hub/g/[groupToken]` (when member has `can_invite` permission)
- Event detail overview tab (circle invite card)
- Chef circles management page

**What remains as permanent exit:**
Opening the native SMS composer to actually send the message. This is permanent and correct, as users should control who they message from their own phone number.

**Priority:** High frequency (every new circle needs invites sent) x Low effort (fully built) = P2 (already well-handled)
**Spec needed?** No. The invite flow is comprehensive. Server-side chef-initiated SMS invite could be a future enhancement.

## Scenario #46: Discuss sensitive guest detail privately

**Original classification:** Reducible
**Reclassified to:** Reducible

**Why guest leaves:** A guest needs to share sensitive information (severe allergy details, accessibility needs, health conditions, personal dietary reasons) that they do not want visible to the entire Circle. They resort to texting or calling the chef directly.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- `GuestPrivateChat` component in `components/hub/guest-private-chat.tsx` provides 1:1 private messaging between a circle member and the chef
- `getOrCreatePrivateThread()` in `lib/hub/private-message-actions.ts` creates private conversation threads within the circle context
- `PrivateThreadChat` component handles the actual message display and input
- Private tab ("Private" with lock emoji) available to authenticated circle members in the hub group view (line 238-240 of hub-group-view.tsx)
- Dietary collection forms (`/dietary-confirm/[token]`) are already private per-guest
- Guest portal messaging (`sendGuestMessage`) exists for event-specific private communication

**Data source?** No. All communication is internal.

**Client-collaborative angle:** The chef is the recipient of the sensitive detail. The system provides the private channel so the guest does not need to find an external way to reach them.

**Physical reality:** Screen-based text input. Sensitive information benefits from written (not verbal) communication for accuracy. Private chat is the correct interface.

**Compounding:** High. Allergy details, accessibility needs, and health conditions persist in the guest's profile and inform all future events with this guest.

**Solution design:**

- Already built: `GuestPrivateChat` component with thread creation and messaging
- Already built: private tab in circle navigation for members with profile tokens
- Already built: dietary forms that are already per-guest and private
- Gap: Private chat is only visible to members who already have a profile token and are on the "Private" tab. It is not prominently surfaced at the moment a guest encounters a "sensitive info" prompt
- Gap: No contextual prompt on dietary/allergy forms saying "Need to share something privately? Start a private conversation with the chef"
- Gap: The private chat requires navigating to a separate tab. A small "Message chef privately" button near sensitive form fields would reduce the exit to text/phone

**Where it appears:**

- `/hub/g/[groupToken]` (Private tab, requires membership)
- Could appear: dietary confirmation pages, guest portal, allergy collection forms

**What remains as permanent exit:**
If the guest has not yet joined the Circle (no profile token), they may still text/call. Also, truly urgent or complex health discussions (e.g., anaphylaxis protocols) may warrant a phone call regardless.

**Priority:** Medium frequency (sensitive details are common but not every guest) x Low effort (feature exists, needs discoverability) = P1
**Spec needed?** No. The feature is built. Work needed: surface private chat prompts near sensitive form fields.

## Scenario #47: Download or save a QR code

**Original classification:** Permanent
**Reclassified to:** Permanent

**Why guest leaves:** After generating or viewing a QR code (for circle join, guest lead, or ticket), the guest needs to download/save it to their device files for printing or sharing. The browser download action places the file in the device's file system, which is outside ChefFlow's surface.

**Context ChefFlow has:**

- `CircleQrCode` component (`components/hub/circle-qr-code.tsx`) generates QR codes client-side using the `qrcode` library with a download link (`<a href={dataUrl} download="dinner-circle-qr.png">Save QR</a>`)
- `GuestCodePanel` (`components/events/guest-code-panel.tsx`) displays QR codes for guest lead capture using an external QR API (`api.qrserver.com`)
- Ticket QR codes served from `/api/tickets/[ticketId]/qr/route.ts`
- QR join URLs generated by `lib/dinner-circles/qr-utils.ts`
- The `showDownload` prop on `CircleQrCode` controls whether the save button appears

**Data source?** Partially. `GuestCodePanel` uses an external QR API (`api.qrserver.com`), while `CircleQrCode` generates locally. The download itself is a browser-native file operation.

**Client-collaborative angle:** Not applicable. QR download is a solo device action.

**Physical reality:** QR codes are inherently physical: they are meant to be printed on cards, displayed on screens at events, or texted as images. The download-to-device step is the bridge between digital creation and physical use.

**Compounding:** Low. Each QR code is a one-time artifact for a specific event or circle. No knowledge compounds from the download action itself.

**Solution design:**

- Already built: client-side QR generation with download link in `CircleQrCode`
- Already built: named download file ("dinner-circle-qr.png") for easy identification
- Gap: `GuestCodePanel` relies on external `api.qrserver.com` rather than local generation (minor reliability concern)
- Gap: No "print-ready" formatting option (larger size, with event name and date as label)
- Gap: No "share QR as image" via Web Share API (would allow texting the QR image directly without save-then-attach)

**Where it appears:**

- Circle QR code display in hub group settings
- Guest code panel on event detail pages
- Ticket confirmation pages (ticket QR)

**What remains as permanent exit:**
The browser file download is a permanent exit. Files land in the device's file system, which ChefFlow cannot control. Printing is also external. These exits are correct and permanent.

**Priority:** Low frequency (QR download is occasional, not daily) x Low effort (already built) = P3
**Spec needed?** No. Minor enhancements (local generation for GuestCodePanel, share-as-image) are small tasks, not spec-worthy.

## Key Findings

**Infrastructure maturity:** This category is remarkably well-built. 5 of 7 scenarios have working code that covers the primary path. The Dinner Circle system has: recovery emails, Web Push, email-to-circle ingestion, private chat, QR generation with download, role-aware invite copy with SMS deep links, and comprehensive notification preferences.

**Primary gaps are discoverability, not capability:**

- Recovery form (#41) exists but only surfaces inside the circle view, not from email deep link failures
- Private chat (#46) exists but is a tab-navigation action, not contextually prompted near sensitive forms
- Email reply ingestion (#43) works for inquiry-linked circles but not community circles

**All scenarios marked NEEDS-DEVELOPER-REVIEW** for chef operational insight on: whether email-to-circle should extend to community circles, whether server-side SMS invites are wanted, and whether the auth gate on circle creation is the right friction level.
